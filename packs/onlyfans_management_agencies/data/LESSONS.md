# Six Lessons from Agency Precedents

**1. Account lock-in fails long-term.** Webcam studios and MCNs controlled creator accounts/IPs (40-50% ownership). Both collapsed when creators found alternatives. Portability and clear exit rights are prerequisite.

**2. High margin cuts are unsustainable.** Studios take 30-70%, MCNs took 15-40% of ad revenue, K-pop early-stage: 80-90%. Models earning $5/8-hour shifts and artists at 2% album sales left immediately. Sustainable: creator must earn livable net income.

**3. Support services beat margin extraction.** Studio71 pivoted to brand deals, tools, merch. Loaded diversifies beyond splits. Colombia's best studios offer psych support. Talent management requires operational excellence, not just revenue shares.

**4. Recruitment demands transparency and guarantees.** Heavy investment (monitors, training, bonuses, guaranteed minimums) wins creators. K-pop now absorbs trainee costs. 18-hour shifts with break fines cause burnout. Honesty on earnings potential is table-stakes.

**5. Scale advantage eroded by platform tools.** Maker, Fullscreen, AwesomenessTV won 2012-2016. By 2018, YouTube/Twitch built creator tools; direct monetization became viable. Compete by serving underserved tiers (emerging creators, regional, niche platforms).

**6. Regulation forces standards.** Colombia courts (2021) ordered labor protections; K-pop lawsuits capped contracts at 7 years. Early self-regulation (transparency, health insurance, dispute protocols) builds equity. Late movers face legal exposure and talent backlash.
